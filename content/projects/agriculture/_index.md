## Motivation

<br/>

TODO Re-read this

Despite humanity's best efforts towards globalization and improving mass-scale production, modern produce has several key flaws. While it is very convenient to go to a supermarket and buy (or even get delivered) everything you need in one go and at any season, products are often unappealing, overpriced, bland-tasting, unhealthy (raised with pesticides and other chemicals) and sometimes grown though ethically or environmentaly questionable practices. Plainly speaking, what you buy is often bad. You might have never noticed it, specially if you have never tried agriculture yourself, but the difference in quality for certain products is huge. 

People have been indulging in small-scale, personal farming since the dawn of humanity and still are; it comes as no suprise that there are many benefits as opposed to buying store alternatives. Aside from making a great hobby and serving as an abstention from constantly sitting in front of a computer (at least for us white collar workers), agriculture and self-production of food provides healtier, often tastier alternatives to those available on most commerces. You get to choose what your plants grow on and skip industrial fertilizers and other chemicals that might be harmful. Buying seeds is often much cheaper than buying fully grown vegetables, meaning that eating healthier will cost little more than some additional time to take care of your parcel. Local stores might even have special crop varieties with redeeming qualities that are not commonly mass-produced.

If you relate with any of these things, you might want to give agriculture a try. While having a large parcel and some weekly spare time is ideal for maintaining a varied garden, you can get started with just a few planters and some free space next to a large window. Another great advantage of agriculture is flexibility and how you can scale production with respect to your space and time constraints. 

## On agriculture

<br/>

I have been raising crops ever since I remember and each harvest has something different. Agriculture is not an exact science and there is a wide variety of factors that can influence the outcomes, both positively or negatively. This non-determinism is sometimes refresing, sometimes frustrating; for newcommers it is often the latter. This page contains some of the oddities and techniques I have learnt over the years in the hope that it will be useful for the reader, even if experienced. Different people have different approaches, I will be sharing mine.

Above are details about each type of crop and fruit that I have grown, as well as some general advice and miscellaneous tips that are tangential to all types of crops. A minimal primer on conservation of produce is also included.

While the methods listed in this page have worked for me, there is no guarantee that they will for you, though it can be a great starting point. Again, many factors can condition the sucess of a harvest. If you have any suggestions, recomendations or experiences, drop me a mail, I would be delighted to hear about them.
